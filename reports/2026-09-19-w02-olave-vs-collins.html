The short version

On paper these are the same player: same projection, same trade value, drafted a round apart. The difference is how each gets to ~14.7 a game. Olave earns it on volume — a high-floor, catch-heavy profile that full PPR rewards. Collins earns it on efficiency and touchdowns — a higher single-week ceiling with a slightly lower floor. Right now Olave is more valuable to this roster purely because he is playing; once Collins has two clean games back, the comparison is a coin flip that tilts toward Collins on ceiling.

2 · Two ways to score 14.7

Profile A · volume

Chris OlaveThe target hog

13 targets in Week 1 on top of a 2025 run with Tyler Shough of 48 catches, 660 yards and 6 TD in 8 games — about six catches and 82 yards per game. The Saints trail a lot, and trailing teams throw, which quietly feeds a WR1's target count.

Floor: high. A 60-yard, no-TD day is still 13–14 points because of the receptions.

Ceiling: comes from yardage, not scores. He has never been the Saints' red-zone target.

Profile B · efficiency + touchdowns

Nico CollinsThe big-play, red-zone target

Fewer catches, more yards per catch, and he is Houston's red-zone target. Week 1 was a textbook Collins line — 7 catches, modest yards, a touchdown. Stroud's offense is the better environment of the two.

Floor: a little lower. A 5-catch, no-TD day is ~10 points where Olave's equivalent is ~14.

Ceiling: higher. Top-5 WR per-game production in 2024 when healthy.

Target share — the fraction of a team's pass attempts thrown to one player. In PPR it is the stickiest predictor of value because every catch is a point regardless of yardage. Olave's is elite; Collins' is high but he converts it into fewer, longer catches.
Grade-1 hamstring strain — the mildest tier (micro-tears, no major fibre damage). Typical return is 1–3 weeks, but hamstrings re-strain easily when a player comes back early, which is why beat writers hedge with "fluid".

3 · What it means for this roster

1

Olave is the more valuable player to you right now, on availability alone. He is the only healthy WR1 while Collins and Pittman occupy both IR slots. The comparison only flips once Collins is back and the hamstring has held for two games.

2

They are complementary, not redundant. In a 2 WR + 2 FLEX lineup you want both: Olave as the safe weekly start, Collins as the ceiling play. Do not treat "which one" as a real question unless a trade forces it.

3

Collins' return forces a cut around Week 4. Both IR slots are full, so activating him means dropping someone. Decide the weakest bench hold now (Bigsby / Allgeier per the Week 2 roster review) rather than on the Tuesday he's cleared.

4

Keeper math is a wash. Both are prime-age veterans at a flat round-skip cost; either is a legitimate keeper if they finish top-12. Neither is the "nearly free" late-round breakout the strategy doc prizes.

5

If one ever has to go: sell Collins only after two healthy games (his price is depressed while he sits), or sell Olave now if a WR1-for-WR1 swap is on offer. Never either of them for a depth package — three roster spots of value is not one roster spot of value when you can only start seven skill players.

Consensus check: industry has both as WR1s in the 8–12 range rest-of-season, with Olave slightly ahead this week only because he is playing. This report agrees.
