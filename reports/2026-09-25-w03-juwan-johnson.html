The short version

Johnson is a low-end TE1 / high-end TE2, and I agree with the TE11 consensus. When healthy he plays nearly every passing down for the NFL's #2-ranked passing offense, and he caught 77 balls on 102 targets last year in this same scheme. That is a real, repeatable role. What caps him is target volume: about 13% of the team's targets, fourth in line behind Olave, Vele and the running backs. Add the return of rookie WR Jordyn Tyson around Weeks 6–7 and Noah Fant still taking a share of snaps, and he looks like a ~10–11 point-per-game player, not a difference-maker.

For The Man Wolf: he would be a modest upgrade over Fannin at TE, worth roughly 1–3 points a week. He also brings an awkward schedule: four tough matchups in a row (Weeks 3–6), then a much softer run that peaks in fantasy playoff Weeks 15–16 (@TB, vs ARI). In the Kittle trade he is the add-on, not the reason to accept. Evans is the reason.

Week 1 is the real role; Week 2 was the illness

Johnson missed Wednesday practice before Week 2 with an illness. That game his routes dropped from 84% to 59%, and Noah Fant (44% of snaps) and extra-lineman-as-TE Asim Richards (41%) absorbed the difference. Even so, Johnson out-targeted Fant 4–0. In the healthy Week 1 game he played 84% of snaps against Fant's 41% and ran 53 routes to Fant's 19. DraftSharks' read, which I share: the two "are not as close as they seem." Fant has 8 targets on 19 routes (a fluky rate that won't hold), and rookie Oscar Delp is now pushing Fant for the TE2 job.

What to watch Sunday vs LV: if Johnson's route share is back above 75%, the Week 1 role is confirmed. If it stays near 60% while he's healthy, this is a real committee and his floor drops by about 2–3 points.

Target share is the share of a team's pass attempts aimed at a player. For TEs, about 15% is solid TE2 volume, about 20% is TE1 volume, and 25%+ is elite (McBride territory). First-read share is how often the QB looks to him first on a play (13.6% for Johnson, per FantasyPros). It shows how the offense is designed, which is stickier than the raw target count.

At 13.1% Johnson sits below the TE1 threshold. He's fourth in the Saints' pecking order: Olave (23 targets), Vele (16), and the RB group (Etienne 11, Kamara 6) all rank ahead of him. What keeps him fantasy-relevant is the environment. Tyler Shough threw 56 times in Week 1 (trailing Detroit) and 34 times in Week 2, in Kellen Moore's up-tempo offense. At roughly 45 passes a game, even a 13% share means 5–6 targets per week. In 2025, in the same scheme, he earned 102 targets (6.0 per game), one of only seven TEs to reach 100.

2 · Efficiency: what he does with the ball

YPRR (yards per route run) is receiving yards divided by routes run. It's the best single efficiency stat for receivers because it counts the snaps where he ran a route and wasn't targeted. Around 1.5 is average for a starting TE, 2.0+ is very good, and 2.5+ is elite. TPRR (targets per route run) measures how well a player earns the ball when he's out running routes. About 20% is good for a TE. YAC is yards after the catch.

Treat the efficiency as regression-prone, especially the YAC

The two weeks tell opposite stories. Week 1: 53 routes, 54 yards (1.02 YPRR), bailed out by a touchdown. Week 2: 23 routes, 66 yards (2.87 YPRR) on 4-of-4 catching, with 55 of the 66 yards after the catch. YAC-driven yardage is the least repeatable kind. His 2025 baseline (77 catches, 889 yards, 11.5 yards per catch) is the better anchor. That's a reliable intermediate target, not an explosive one. Expect the season's 17.1 yards per catch to drift toward about 11–12.

FantasyPros adds a matchup-specific note: against zone coverage (defenses guarding areas rather than following individual receivers), Johnson's efficiency drops slightly to 1.60 YPRR / 12% TPRR. Las Vegas plays zone 85.3% of the time, which is why the Primer calls him a "low-end TE1 this week," not a smash play.

3 · What that's worth in our scoring

In D League a TE scores 1 point per catch, 0.1 per yard and 6 per TD, with no TE-premium bonus. A reliable 4–5 catches with ~50 yards is worth about 9–10 points before any touchdown. Johnson's 2025 line (77-889-3) converts to 10.8 points per game here. Take away his one 2026 touchdown and he'd be averaging 9.5. The 6-point TD (24% of his 25 points this season) is the swing factor.

What the calendar means

Weeks 3–6 are his worst stretch. All four opponents held TEs to fewer than 9 half-PPR points a game in 2025. That's also exactly when Tyson is due back (a 2-month hamstring injury from Aug 17, IR-designated to return, so about Week 6–7). Expect his production to look ordinary through early October, and don't read too much into it.

Weeks 7–16 flip the script. Six of the nine games are neutral or better, including CIN (#32) and back-to-back fantasy playoff games against TB (#28) and ARI (#31). If The Man Wolf makes the playoffs, those are the two weeks a TE matters most. This is why Johnson's value for us rises later in the year, and it runs opposite to Kittle, who SF is building up to peak late.

5 · Upside and risk

Upside drivers

  • Every-down role when healthy. 84% route share in Week 1, locked in by a $21M guaranteed contract and 2025's 100-target season with this QB and play-caller.
  • Pass volume. Saints rank #2 in passing, with 90 attempts in two games. Game scripts where they're trailing (bad defense, shootouts) add more.
  • Red-zone role. 4 red-zone targets in 2 games. Only 3 TDs last year means touchdowns are the obvious place for positive regression (a stat drifting back toward its normal level). Every TD is a 6-point swing, about 60% of a median week.
  • Softer second half. CIN, TB and ARI all fall in weeks that matter.

Risk drivers

  • Jordyn Tyson (8th overall pick, WR) returns around Weeks 6–7. A first-round WR will take targets, most likely from the intermediate middle of the field where Johnson works. It hurts Olave's volume a little too.
  • TE committee. Fant (44% of snaps in W2) and rookie Delp keep snaps in play, and Moore uses heavy sets with Richards as an extra TE.
  • Efficiency will regress. 17.1 yards per catch and 55 YAC in Week 2 won't last. The baseline is about 11.5.
  • TD dependence. Without the Week 1 TD he'd be averaging 9.5, basically replacement level.
  • Concentration. With Olave and Kamara, that's three Saints for The Man Wolf, so a bad Shough day hits all three. Week 8 bye collides with SF.
  • No keeper value. He's 30. Under our flat round-skip keeper cost, he's never worth a keeper slot.

6 · Fit with The Man Wolf and our strategy

Strategy check (strategy.md): "TE: mid-tier is fine … not worth premium assets outside the top handful." Johnson is exactly that mid-tier TE, and in the proposed trade he costs us nothing extra: Evans alone outvalues Kittle on both trade charts (Yahoo 36 vs 22). The same doc says bench spots should go to "RB/WR handcuffs and high-ceiling young players … over a backup QB or a second TE." So we should not carry Johnson and Fannin long-term.

Recommended usage if acquired:

  • Week 3 (vs ALI): close to a coin flip with Fannin. ESPN projects Fannin 9.8 (vs CAR, a soft #21 matchup) against Johnson 9.2 (vs LV, #4). Consensus ranks Johnson TE9 and Fannin TE14. I lean Johnson on role (84% routes vs Fannin's 20% target share). The trade may not even clear before Sunday, so settle this in /weekly-lineup.
  • Weeks 4–7: starting TE by default, with a low bar. Watch his route share and Tyson's activation.
  • Week 8 (bye, vs 1st-place Lamar n Order): Fannin (CLE plays) covers TE. That's a reason to keep Fannin through Week 8. Afterward, Fannin becomes the cut candidate if we need the roster spot for RB/WR depth (Collins' IR return or a waiver RB). Or keep Fannin over Johnson if his 20% target share holds, since he's 22 and has cheap keeper optionality under our flat round-skip keeper cost.
  • Weeks 15–16: lock him in. @TB and vs ARI are his two best playoff-window matchups.

League angle: Johnson is currently a bench piece for Imad, behind Kraft. Taking him doesn't strengthen Imad's lineup for our Week 5 or Week 14 meetings. It removes Imad's TE insurance, which makes Kittle more essential to Imad's lineup than to ours.
