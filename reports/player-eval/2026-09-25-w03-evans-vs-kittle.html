1 · Role and volume

Snap share is the percentage of the offense's plays a player is on the field for. It's the ceiling on everything else: you can't get targets on the sideline. Target share is the percentage of the team's pass attempts thrown to him. Around 20% is a WR2/WR1 role; around 15% is solid for a TE. Targets per snap shows how often he's thrown to when he's out there, which is a good read on a player whose snaps are capped.

Evans has the bigger role; Kittle has the better per-snap rate and room to grow

Healthy Evans is an every-down outside receiver. He played 75% of snaps in W1 and tied Deebo for the WR target lead. That matches his track record: DraftSharks/PFF put his 2025 target share at 24.4%, with a 30.6% first-read share (how often he's the QB's first option) in seven full games.

Kittle is still on a managed count. The ledger's earlier note that his snaps were "lifting" needs a correction: he went from 46% to 48%, which is flat, not a ramp. W2 was a 35–13 blowout where Luke Farrell (54%) took the blocking snaps. Kittle's per-snap target rate (0.16) is higher than Evans' (0.13), though. If the count lifts toward his usual ~80%, the same rate would mean about 7 targets a game. RotoBaller notes he was the overall TE2 over Weeks 8–16 last year once fully healthy. Experts agree the ramp is coming (NBC, RotoBaller). Nobody has a date.

Shared ceiling: SF threw 34 and 22 passes. In a run-first offense that keeps winning big, CMC, Deebo, Evans and Kittle split a small pie. W3 helps both because Demarcus Robinson is OUT.

6 · Fit with The Man Wolf and our strategy

Strategy check: strategy.md says "WR: volume over ceiling," "TE: mid-tier is fine unless a true elite (top-3) TE can be had cheaply," and "win-now." Kittle isn't elite by consensus (TE6–TE9), so strategy wouldn't pay for him. But it doesn't say to sell a TE1 for a smaller lineup total either. Win-now means weekly starting points come first.

  • Where each plays: Kittle is effectively our TE1, with Fannin as the fallback. Evans would be a FLEX. Neither changes our NFL concentration, since we'd still hold exactly one 49ers pass-catcher.
  • WR depth argument for Evans: Collins (hamstring; ESPN now lists him Q for W3, not OUT, so confirm in /weekly-lineup) and Pittman (foot, IR) are both banged up. Evans would be insurance at WR. If Collins returns on schedule, that argument weakens.
  • Roster cost: a 1-for-1 swap costs nothing. The actual 2-for-1 offer needs a cut (cut order from the ledger: Allgeier → Harvey → Worthy → Pittman).
  • League angle: Imad (1-1, 5th) is our W5 and W14 opponent. Sending Kittle strengthens his TE slot (Kraft today) for both of those games.
  • Keeper: neither has keeper value (Evans 33, Kittle 33 in Oct). The tiebreaker doesn't apply.

Why Kittle wins, and what would flip it

Why: Same offense, same bye, similar volume, a near-equal calendar, and near-equal ESPN projections. The tiebreaker is our roster. Kittle keeps Fannin out of the starting lineup, and Evans only replaces a ~11-pt FLEX. Kittle also has a clean injury report today, while Evans is Q at 33.

Flip to Evans if any of these happen: (1) Kittle's snap share stays under ~55% through W5 (the ramp isn't coming). (2) Fannin holds a 20%+ target share for another 2 weeks, which would close the TE gap. (3) Collins or Pittman suffers a setback that leaves us short at WR. (4) Evans plays through the hip in W3 at 75%+ snaps. Any two of these and Evans becomes the better piece for us.

For the pending trade: this report doesn't reject it. With Johnson included, the deal trades about 1 pt/week of starting lineup for depth at WR and TE and a big market-value surplus. It's a closer call than "ACCEPT" suggested. If you want the weekly points, hold Kittle; if you'd rather have insurance for Collins' hamstring and Pittman's foot, take it.
