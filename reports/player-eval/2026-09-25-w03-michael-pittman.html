The short version

WR4/5 depth: a bye-week flex, not a starter. Pittman is the Steelers' clear WR2 behind DK Metcalf and plays nearly every snap when healthy. But the rest of his situation caps him: Pittsburgh's offense has been one of the league's worst through two weeks (264 and 235 total yards, 23 points combined), Aaron Rodgers is averaging 5.1 yards per attempt, and in his one game Pittman drew only 3 targets. His ROS schedule is also rough: 7 of his 11 remaining regular-season games come against 2025 top-10 WR defenses. His 2025 profile (21% target share, 1.90 YPRR) says the 3-target game is too low to be his true level. The offense and schedule say he won't get far above ~10 points a week either.

For The Man Wolf: he sits behind Olave, Collins (back ~W4) and Jameson Williams, roughly level with Worthy. His real job is cover in W5 (Worthy's bye, vs IND, the 2nd-softest WR defense in 2025) and W8 (Olave, Collins, Kittle and Kamara all on bye). Hold him, but he's the 3rd/4th name on the cut list, not untouchable. A healthy Courtland Sutton is sitting on our waiver wire and consensus ranks him higher.

Locked-in role, thin target volume

The role isn't in question. He played 88% of snaps and ran a route on nearly every dropback in W1, and when he sat, a rookie (Bernard) took his snaps rather than someone overtaking him. The teammates due back or arriving don't threaten him either. Wilson and Bernard compete for the slot, while Pittman and Metcalf play outside.

The issue is what the role produces. Pittsburgh throws ~37 targets a game, and Metcalf takes a quarter of them. Three targets in W1 is probably a floor, not the norm: it's one game, and he'd been limited by a separate injury through camp. But even a 17–18% share, a realistic number next to Metcalf, is ~6.5 targets a game in an offense averaging 4.7–5.5 yards per pass. Experts agree on the role. The Yahoo Outlook says he'd "reclaim a significant role from Bernard" and calls him a flex. No one frames him as a weekly starter.

Watch next: his target share in his first two healthy games. ≥15% supports the ESPN projection; under 12% again means the W1 number wasn't noise.

2 · Efficiency

YPRR (yards per route run) is receiving yards divided by routes run, the best single measure of how productive a receiver is per chance. 2.0+ is very good, ~1.5 average, under 1.2 poor. TPRR (targets per route run) is how often he's thrown to when he runs a route; ~20% is typical for a starting WR. First-read share is how often he's the QB's first option on a play. YAC is yards after the catch.

Both W1 numbers will regress, in opposite directions

His W1 target rate (~7%) should rise toward his baseline: a 21% target share and 24.5% first-read share in 2025 (FantasyPros). His W1 per-target efficiency (19.3 yds/target, 43% of yards after the catch) should fall. He's a possession receiver: career-best 72.1% catch rate in 2025 but only 9.8 yards per catch and 3.4 YAC per catch. Put those together and you get roughly 5 catches for 50–55 yards on a normal day. That's his 2025 shape, minus the offensive quality.

The FantasyPros 2026 outlook is a warning on the quarterback link: Pittman was the WR9 in fantasy points per game through W10 of 2025 with a functioning QB, then topped 40 yards once after Daniel Jones got hurt. Rodgers at 4.7–5.5 yards per attempt is closer to the second half of 2025 than the first.

5 · Upside and risk

Upside drivers

  • Full-time outside role (88% snaps, ~91% routes) with no real competitor. Bernard and Wilson fight for the slot.
  • Proven target earner: 21% share, 24.5% first-read share in 2025, and 109 catches in 2023. The W1 3-target game is well below his norm.
  • Metcalf is catching only 8 of 19 targets. If Rodgers' accuracy on Metcalf stays poor, some volume drifts to the higher-percentage option, which is Pittman.
  • Friendly fantasy-playoff schedule (W15 BAL, W17 TEN).

Risk drivers

  • Durability: two different injuries since Aug 8 (camp, then foot), still limited all week. Playing through foot injuries at WR often means reduced snaps on return. Unconfirmed whether he'll be on a snap count.
  • Offense: PIT scored 3 points in W2; Rodgers at 4.7–5.5 Y/A; 19.5-point implied total in W3. His 2025 production collapsed once the QB play did.
  • Schedule: 7 of 11 regular-season games vs top-10 WR defenses.
  • TD dependence: 21% of 2025 points came from a career-high 7 TDs (his prior two seasons: 4 and 3).

6 · Fit with The Man Wolf and our strategy

Strategy check: "WR: volume over ceiling" favors his archetype, a possession receiver with a high catch rate, but the volume in Pittsburgh belongs to Metcalf. "Youth as a bench tiebreaker" works against him versus Worthy (23). Pittman is 28 with no keeper case under the flat round-skip cost. He was a R10 pick, so there's no sunk-cost reason to hold.

  • Where he fits: WR5 behind Olave, Collins (back ~W4) and Jameson Williams, and roughly level with Worthy (FP WR55 vs WR53; Boone 14 vs 12; ESPN 11.89 vs 11.01). He doesn't start over Kittle or Tuten at FLEX in a normal week.
  • The weeks he earns his spot: W5 (Worthy bye, vs IND) and above all W8, when Olave, Collins, Kittle and Kamara are all off. That week our WRs would be Jameson Williams, Worthy, Washington and Pittman (plus Evans if the Kittle trade goes through, but SF is also on bye W8). In W6, J. Williams and Washington are both off.
  • The roster crunch arrives W4 no matter what: once he's cleared he must leave IR (it's already technically illegal while he's Q), and Collins comes off IR ~W4. That's two cuts, three with the 2-for-1 Kittle trade. The ledger's cut order still holds: Allgeier → Harvey → Worthy → Pittman. But Worthy vs Pittman is a near coin flip on consensus, and youth favors Worthy. Decision rule: if Pittman is under 12% target share in his first two healthy games (W3–W4), he drops below Worthy.
  • League angle: Metcalf (Get Hooked, our W7 opponent) is the only other PIT pass-catcher rostered in-league, so no stack concerns. Pittman has little trade value (Boone 14), so at most he's a throw-in to balance a deal.
