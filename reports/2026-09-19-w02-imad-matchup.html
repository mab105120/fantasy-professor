The three spots, argued

1. Purdy over Lawrence — Purdy. +4.7 proj

Lawrence had the better Week 1 (26.1 vs 21.1), which is why this is worth a sentence. The spread settles it: SF is a 12.5-point home favorite over a Malik Willis-led Dolphins team, Jacksonville is a 2.5-point road dog in Denver. Big favorites throw efficiently for three quarters and then run out the clock; that's a 20-point median with a low floor. Lawrence's path to beating that is a shootout with Bo Nix, who was terrible in W1. Purdy, and it agrees with every major W2 ranking.

2. WR — Evans and Watson; London holds. wash on paper

Three receivers project within half a point (Evans 13.0, London 12.9, Watson 12.5). When projections tie, decide on the offense he's attached to. Evans has Purdy at home as a huge favorite. Watson has Jordan Love in a pass-heavy script with Jacobs unavailable and just posted 32.7. London has Cooper Rush, who completed 12 passes last week. London is the most talented of the three and the one you drafted in round 3 — he'll be a starter again the moment Atlanta has a quarterback. This week he isn't.

3. Flex — Skattebo over Judkins. +0.9 proj, bigger floor gap

Judkins is the better back on a worse team. Cleveland is an 8.5-point road underdog with an offense that generated four pressures' worth of protection and 7.0 points from Judkins last week; trailing teams abandon the run. The Giants are 7-point dogs too, but they're run-first by design and Skattebo's 14.1 came against Dallas. Skattebo also plays Monday night, which gives you a live piece after everything else is in — that only matters if the game is close, but it's a free option.

Glossary

Projection (proj)
ESPN's median expected fantasy points for the week under this league's scoring (PPR, 4-pt passing TD). A median means the player beats it about as often as not; team totals land within ~25 of the sum about two-thirds of the time.
Spread / total
Spread = how many points the sportsbook favorite is expected to win by. Total (O/U) = expected combined score. Big favorites run late (good for RBs, bad for their QB's 4th quarter); high totals mean more fantasy points to go around.
Stack
Starting a QB and his own receiver (Purdy–Evans) or RB and receiver (Walker–Rice). Their points move together — higher ceiling, higher variance.
Game script
How the flow of the game shapes usage: a trailing team throws (good for its WRs, bad for its RB), a leading team runs.
Yards per route run (YPRR)
Receiving yards divided by pass routes run — an efficiency stat that strips out how often the team throws. 2.0+ is starter territory; Burden's 2.79 in W1 is excellent.
Power score
ESPN's team-strength metric, a two-game rolling average of scoring that ignores win/loss.
Q / Doubtful / IR
Questionable = uncertain, usually plays (Burrow); Doubtful = usually sits (Bowers, Tua); IR = out at least 4 games. Inactives are announced 90 minutes before kickoff.
