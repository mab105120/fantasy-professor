The three decisions, argued

1. Olave over Worthy — start Olave if he's active. +4.9 proj

Olave is a target-share monster (13 targets in W1) in a full-PPR league, which is exactly the profile this roster is built around. The injury is the only question, and every reputable read on it (Saints' official report, NBC, FantasyPros, NewOrleans.Football's beat reporter) says precautionary and trending toward playing. The sequencing makes this low-risk: NO–BAL is an early game, KC–IND is Sunday night. If Olave is on the inactive list at 11:30 CT, drop Worthy back in — you lose nothing. If Olave is active but you'd rather not carry the hamstring risk, that's a judgment call I'd make the other way: a limited Olave still commands targets; Worthy is a deep-ball dice roll (3 catches on a team-high target share last week).

Consensus check: Olave is a WR2 in every major W2 ranking when active; Worthy sits WR50–55 (RotoBaller) and is a CBS "sit". This recommendation agrees with consensus.

2. Dak over Stafford — keep Dak. +1.8 proj

Both are start-worthy this week (SI, RotoBaller, CBS all say start both), so the question is which matchup is softer. Washington allowed the 5th-most passing yards per game last season and ranked 31st against fantasy QBs; Dak put up 23.3 and 22.7 against them in two 2025 meetings. Stafford's 4.1 in W1 was a dud, and the Giants just held Dallas down with the Cover-2 looks that gave Stafford his worst efficiency last year. Small edge, Dak. Agrees with consensus.

3. Flex — Tuten and Kittle; Fannin at TE. Harvey stays out

Four players project within half a point of each other (Kittle 10.5, Tuten 10.1, Harvey 10.0, Fannin 10.0), so this is about floor and availability. Harvey is a game-time decision in a committee — the only one of the four who can hand you a zero. Kittle was cleared Friday and his snap count should climb against a Miami defense that leaks to tight ends; Tuten leads the JAX backfield in a matchup Kenneth Walker just exposed. Fannin's usage dip (4th on CLE in targets) is real and the consensus is genuinely split on him, but the alternative at TE is Kittle, who's already in the flex — so Fannin at TE, Kittle at flex is the same lineup either way. The only true alternative is Malik Washington (7.5) or Kamara (5.4), and neither beats a healthy Kittle or Tuten.

Glossary

Projection (proj)
ESPN's median expected fantasy points for the week under this league's scoring. A median means the player beats it about as often as not; team totals are usually within ~25 points of the sum.
Target share
The fraction of a team's pass attempts thrown to one player. In PPR, targets are the most stable predictor of week-to-week scoring because each catch is a point before any yardage.
Snap %
Share of the team's offensive plays a player was on the field for. Opportunity precedes production; a rising snap count (Kittle) is the leading indicator.
Committee
A backfield where carries are split among 2–3 backs (DEN: Harvey/Dobbins/Coleman). Committee backs have low floors because the game script picks who gets the work.
Locked
A player whose game has kicked off can't be moved. Jameson Williams (Thu) and both teams' Thursday players are final; Kincaid's 22.5 is locked on their bench.
Power score
ESPN's team-strength metric, a two-game rolling average of scoring that ignores win/loss — used to spot teams stronger or weaker than their record.
Points-for (PF)
Season total scored. The playoff tiebreaker after record in this league.
Q / Questionable
Official NFL designation meaning uncertain to play. Inactives are announced 90 minutes before kickoff — that's when a Q resolves.
